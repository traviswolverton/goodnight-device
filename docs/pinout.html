MCP23017 bus connector

The Waveshare board repeats the same six signals on its 6-pin locking connector and on a row of through-holes along one edge, in this order: VCC, GND, SDA, SCL, INTA, INTB. The stock cable is red, black, blue, yellow, orange, green in that order. Solder pads A0, A1 and A2 set the address; leave all three as shipped for 0x27 (confirmed on a real board: the scan found it at 0x27). The diagram groups pins by function, so wire by the printed labels, not by the drawing’s order.

Hub board

Every shared connection goes through one stripboard, so nothing is soldered pin to pin. The board has 15 copper strips, each one net, with no cuts. Each device gets a JST-XH header laid across the strips it needs, so its cable plugs straight in. 3V3 and GND each appear on several strips, joined by wire links on top, so every header stays within a few strips of the rails it needs. The last strip, s14, is ground for the eight buttons: a row of eight pins laid along it, tied together by the strip itself. Wiring only: where the board sits is in the enclosure.

Reading the table: the hub end is a JST-XH plug onto a header soldered into the hub, except the ESP32’s wires (soldered to the strips) and the button ground (a plain pin header along s14). The device end depends on what the module has. A is a crimped Dupont-style housing on the module’s 2.54 mm pins (easiest, but a friction fit). B is wires soldered straight to the pins or leads. C is the stock pin header desoldered and an XH header soldered in its place, which gives a latching connector at both ends.

Wire colours

The same signal is the same colour at both ends of every cable, and no two wires in one plug share a colour. Red is always 3V3 and black is always GND. A colour can repeat on different cables when they never share a plug: white is the DHT11’s data and the stick’s press, and yellow is SCL and the lid’s button signals. The I2C and expander colours follow the expander’s stock cable. The ESP32’s ten wires are all different, so they are easy to tell apart in the two housings.

Buttons and their ground

The buttons’ signals don’t go through the hub: each has its own wire to the expander’s PA header. Their shared ground does. Strip s14 on the hub is tied to GND by the GND link and has a 1×8 male header laid along it; the eight pins are joined by the strip, so nothing is bridged with solder. The lid’s eight ground wires end in an 8-way housing that plugs onto that header, so the whole lid unplugs: that housing, the signal housing on the PA header, the stick’s plug on the hub, and the one-wire press plug. The joystick’s press switch is the one other wire to the expander, to PB0; its other side is the stick’s GND, which comes from the hub.

Pins left alone

Avoid GPIO0, 2, 12 and 15 (they set how the chip boots), 1 and 3 (the USB serial) and 6–11 (the flash). Free for later: 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 23, 26, 32 and 33. The joystick has to stay on 34/35: analog reads need ADC1 (GPIO32–39) because ADC2 stops working while Wi-Fi is on.

Wiring order: power first. Join every 3V3 pin to the ESP32’s 3V3 and every ground to GND, and check both with a multimeter before anything else is connected. Then add the I2C parts one at a time (OLED, BH1750, MCP23017) and look for [i2c] found: 0x23 0x27 0x3C on the serial monitor, then the buttons, joystick, DHT11 and piezo. Put the BH1750 where it sees the room, not the screen.
