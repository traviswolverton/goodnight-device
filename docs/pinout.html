MCP23017 bus connector

The Waveshare board repeats the same six signals on its 6-pin locking connector and on a row of through-holes along one edge, in this order: VCC, GND, SDA, SCL, INTA, INTB. The stock cable is red, black, blue, yellow, orange, green in that order. Solder pads A0, A1 and A2 set the address; leave all three as shipped for 0x27 (confirmed on a real board: the scan found it at 0x27). The diagram groups pins by function, so wire by the printed labels, not by the drawing’s order.

Bus board

Only four things are shared: GND, 3V3, SDA and SCL. They go through one small stripboard (24 × 55 mm) with a screw-terminal block soldered along one copper strip for each bus, so a bus is just a row of screws that are the same net. Everything else is a plain Dupont jumper straight from the ESP32 (or the expander) to its device. Any free screw on a bus will do, so the labels below are only one way to fill them. Wiring only: where the board sits is in the enclosure.

Wire colours

The same signal is the same colour at both ends of its wire. Red is always 3V3 and black is always GND. A colour can repeat when the wires never sit side by side: white is the DHT11’s data and the stick’s press, and yellow is SCL and the lid’s button signals. The I2C and expander colours follow the expander’s stock cable.

Buttons and their ground

Each button has its own yellow signal wire to the expander’s PA header. Their grounds are joined to each other on the lid, and that one common wire goes to a single GND screw on the bus board, so the buttons use one of the ten GND wires, not eight. On the PA header, bend the pins flat so a plug goes on sideways: the header is PA7 at the top down to PA0, then GND and VCC at the bottom, so button n goes to the pin labelled PAn−1. The joystick’s press switch is the one other wire to the expander, to PB0; its other side is the stick’s GND, from the bus board.

Pins left alone

Avoid GPIO0, 2, 12 and 15 (they set how the chip boots), 1 and 3 (the USB serial) and 6–11 (the flash). Free for later: 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 23, 26, 32 and 33. The joystick has to stay on 34/35: analog reads need ADC1 (GPIO32–39) because ADC2 stops working while Wi-Fi is on.

Wiring order: power first. Join every 3V3 pin to the ESP32’s 3V3 and every ground to GND, and check both with a multimeter before anything else is connected. Then add the I2C parts one at a time (OLED, BH1750, MCP23017) and look for [i2c] found: 0x23 0x27 0x3C on the serial monitor, then the buttons, joystick, DHT11 and buzzer. Put the BH1750 where it sees the room, not the screen.
